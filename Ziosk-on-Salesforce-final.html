The central question

How does Ziosk grow its restaurant footprint without growing operational complexity and cost at the same rate?

  • Governed AI could keep new tools and workflows consistent as more teams adopt them.
  • AI-assisted restaurant support could help operators resolve common issues faster, with complex incidents routed to the support workflow you already run.
  • Neither depends on changing Ziosk's CRM or ticketing platform.

Where Salesforce could contribute, and where it can't.

Salesforce capability

For AI that runs in Salesforce

  • Einstein Trust Layer. Built-in controls on how prompts and data reach a model, such as data masking and audit logging. Coverage varies by feature.
  • Governance and observability for Agentforce agents. Designed in, for agents built on Salesforce.
  • Claude inside Salesforce's trust boundary. Anthropic's models, hosted via Amazon Bedrock, are offered inside Agentforce to select customers.
Needs integration or configuration

Across more of Ziosk's stack

  • MuleSoft Agent Fabric. A separate product described as a control plane to discover, govern, orchestrate, and observe agents, APIs, and MCP servers. Orchestration covers agents that support the A2A standard. Fit with Ziosk's environment would need technical validation.
  • Connections to Jira and Microsoft 365. Would be scoped and built, not assumed.
Ziosk's own governance work

No single vendor covers this

  • Approval policy and vendor risk review for AI tools.
  • Employee use of AI inside Microsoft 365 and other SaaS tools.
  • Usage, cost, and effectiveness reporting across vendors.
  • Acceptable-use rules and model evaluation criteria.
To be clear: Salesforce controls apply to AI running on, or connected through, Salesforce. We are not suggesting Salesforce provides one permission model or one audit trail across every third-party AI tool. Those tools keep their own controls, and Ziosk's governance framework has to span them.

Opportunity B. AI-assisted restaurant operations and support.Hypothesis

Ziosk supports restaurant operators and their teams, not the millions of diners who use the tablets each week. The idea: let approved troubleshooting knowledge help staff resolve common issues, and route complex incidents into the Jira-centered support workflow you already have. Support volume, resolution rates, and cost impact are unknown, and we would not assume them.

What we would want to learn first

  • Which restaurant issues recur most, and who handles them today.
  • Whether the knowledge needed to resolve them is documented and current.
  • How a handoff into Jira Service Management would need to work.
  • What “good” looks like to Ziosk operations leadership.
A comparison worth studying · OpenTable

A restaurant-facing support agent, built on real conversations.

OpenTable also supports restaurants. It started with a restaurant-facing Agentforce agent and reported a 40% improvement in resolution rate versus its previous tool. Salesforce also reports an estimated 70% of Tier 1 restaurant support cases deflected and $2M saved annually.

Its published lessons are instructive: it used real chat transcripts to choose use cases, tightened its knowledge articles, and had the agent open a full-context case when a person was needed.

OpenTable's volume, tools, and knowledge base differ from Ziosk's. We don't expect Ziosk to match its results. Salesforce also acquired Fin, a packaged support agent it describes as working with existing help desks, with a reported 76% average resolution rate across its customers. Whether either approach fits Ziosk is what discovery would test.

Partner-led sales assistance and device-health intelligence are possible later conversations. They are not part of this one.

Historical customer story · Toast

The POS partner Bryan named, on Salesforce in 2019.

In a 2019 Salesforce release, Toast described running its sales and service interactions on Salesforce as it scaled, including quoting, mobile install work, and a customer support hub. It cited 148% revenue growth in 2018 as the reason it outgrew spreadsheets.

This is a six-year-old public story. We have not verified how Toast uses Salesforce today. It illustrates that a fast-growing restaurant-technology company needed systems built for scale, and Ziosk can draw its own conclusions.

What we are not proposing.

A new CRM. Replacing HubSpot, which your sales team recommitted to in May. Replacing Jira Service Management. Changing how Microsoft 365 and Teams are used. We also heard that Ziosk's remaining Salesforce application has been replaced and will likely be retired; this brief stands apart from that.

Salesforce is also working with Microsoft on Copilot and Teams integrations, announced Nov 17, 2026. They focus on Salesforce data and sales workflows, so their relevance to Ziosk is unproven.
